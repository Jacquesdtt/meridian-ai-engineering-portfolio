01 / THE THINKINGSTART WITH THE DECISION

THE PROBLEM

A warning sign
needs a response.

A retention team has more customers than time. A prediction helps only if someone can understand it and act on it.

01

Make the list useful.

Use past account activity to find customers who may leave. Set a priority level to reduce the number of cases sent for review.

02

Make the recommendation checkable.

Show the factors behind a prediction. Let reviewers ask questions about the written project report and inspect the source.

03

Make the decision accountable.

Check offer limits, require review acknowledgement, and save the human decision. Sending an email stays outside the system.

Follow the application guide ↗
  1. 01

    Score the monthly record.

    Select “Flagged (create draft)”. The example scores about 0.751, above the 0.70055 cut. This is a ranking score, not a 75.1% chance of leaving.

  2. 02

    Create a pending draft.

    The service checks the score again before creating the draft. In offline mode, a local template supplies the text and the source is marked “stub”. The offered rate cannot exceed 3.5%.

  3. 03

    Review and approve.

    In the Retention agent view, read and, if needed, edit the pending draft. Acknowledge the review, then approve. The approved draft cannot be edited or decided again.

  4. 04

    Inspect the record.

    The audit trail records creation and approval. Approval sends no email; contact must happen outside this application. No customer response or money retained is demonstrated.

03 / THE EVIDENCEHISTORICAL TEST / SEP 2017 — APR 2020

WHAT THE TEST SHOWED

The result.
And its limits.

The model ranked monthly cases better than a simple rule. At the chosen priority level, however, it missed most cases that met the next-month churn rule.

Read the full evaluation ↗

What is one case?

One customer in one observation month: for example, April activity predicts the May state. A positive outcome means May has a balance that rounds to zero and no active transactions.

The same customer can appear in several months and can meet this rule repeatedly. The figures count monthly records, not unique people, first departures or closed accounts.

THE TRADE-OFF

A shorter list means less review work, but more missed positive cases. The goal of 40% correct flags was not met.

These figures measure predictions, not customers persuaded to stay. Errors also differed across age and location groups. There is no measured retention gain.

Methods, scores & fairness checks +

Training used earlier months; testing used 505,863 later customer-month records. Inputs stop at the observation month; the label checks the following month. Eligible records have at least two months of tenure and a known following month. The rule tests a state, so it can include customers already at zero and inactive.

PR-AUC, a measure of ranking across selection cuts, was 0.219 for XGBoost versus 0.151 for the rule baseline. ROC-AUC was 0.723 versus 0.551.

The displayed precision (22.9%) and recall (9.0%) use a fixed threshold of 0.70055, selected from the validation top 5% of scores. Future queue size can change. Scores are not calibrated probabilities.

Equalized odds difference was about 0.156 for age and 0.318 for state on the recorded sample. This measures differences in error rates between groups. Fairness gaps remain.
